Diffusion & Flow Models

A GAN learns to fake a sound in one leap; a diffusion model learns something humbler and stronger — how to remove a little noise. Stack a thousand such steps and static resolves into a violin. This part derives denoising diffusion from the forward noising process to the reverse sampler, connects it to score matching and continuous flows, and confronts its one real weakness — speed. Three live demos throughout.

1The forward process — destroy a signal on a schedule

Diffusion turns generation inside out. Instead of learning to build a sound from nothing, it defines a fixed forward process that takes a clean signal $\mathbf{x}_0$ and, over $T$ steps, drowns it in Gaussian noise until nothing but static remains. The generative model never has to learn this — it is hand-designed. All the model learns is how to walk it backwards.

Each forward step adds a pinch of noise and shrinks the signal slightly, governed by a variance schedule $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_T$ with $0<\beta_t\ll 1$:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\!\big(\mathbf{x}_t;\ \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\ \beta_t\mathbf{I}\big)$$

The magic is that you never need to iterate. Because a composition of Gaussians is Gaussian, the marginal at any step has a closed form in terms of $\mathbf{x}_0$ alone. Writing $\alpha_t = 1-\beta_t$ and $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s$, a single reparameterisation samples $\mathbf{x}_t$ directly:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0)=\mathcal{N}\!\big(\mathbf{x}_t;\ \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t)\mathbf{I}\big)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf{x}_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon,\ \ \boldsymbol\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf I)$$

Drag the timestep in the demo. At $t=0$ you hear and see the clean tone; slide right and watch $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ fade the signal while $\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}$ raises the noise floor, until the waveform is indistinguishable from white noise. Then press Denoise and watch the process run in reverse — from pure static back to the tone, one small step at a time — and hear the recovered sound.

Forward: slide t to add noise per the cosine schedule. Reverse: press Denoise to sample x₀ back from pure noise, then hear it.

Why this is a good idea. The forward process manufactures, for free, an endless supply of (noisy input, known clean target) pairs at every noise level. That converts an impossible density-estimation problem into a mundane supervised regression: given a noisy signal and its noise level $t$, predict what was added. No adversary, no likelihood integral — just a squared error.

derivation · the closed form q(x_t | x_0)

Apply the recursion and the reparameterisation trick twice. With $\boldsymbol\epsilon_{t-1},\boldsymbol\epsilon'\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbf I)$ independent,

$$\mathbf{x}_t=\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_{t-1}=\sqrt{\alpha_t}\big(\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\mathbf{x}_{t-2}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\boldsymbol\epsilon_{t-2}\big)+\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_{t-1}.$$

The two noise terms are independent Gaussians; their sum has variance $\alpha_t(1-\alpha_{t-1})+(1-\alpha_t)=1-\alpha_t\alpha_{t-1}$, so they merge into one Gaussian of that variance. Unrolling all the way to $\mathbf{x}_0$ telescopes the product of $\alpha$'s into $\bar\alpha_t$:

$$\mathbf{x}_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon.$$

This is why diffusion training is cheap: to make a sample at level $t$ you draw one $\boldsymbol\epsilon$ and one multiply-add — never $t$ sequential steps. As $\bar\alpha_t\!\to\!0$ the signal term vanishes and $\mathbf{x}_T\!\approx\!\boldsymbol\epsilon$, a standard normal with no dependence on the data — which is exactly why we can start reverse sampling from pure noise.

2The reverse process — one network, every noise level

Reversing the chain means sampling from $q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t)$. That true reverse is intractable — it depends on the whole data distribution — but for small $\beta_t$ it is provably close to Gaussian, so we approximate it with a learned Gaussian $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t)=\mathcal N(\mathbf{x}_{t-1};\ \boldsymbol\mu_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t),\ \Sigma_t)$. The only thing the network has to produce is that mean.

Ho et al. (2020) found the parameterisation that makes it work. Rather than predict the mean directly, predict the noise $\boldsymbol\epsilon$ that was added — a network $\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)$. The posterior mean then falls out in closed form, and the training objective collapses to a plain denoising regression, weighted uniformly across timesteps (the celebrated "simple" loss):

$$\mathcal{L}_\text{simple}=\mathbb{E}_{t,\,\mathbf{x}_0,\,\boldsymbol\epsilon}\Big[\big\lVert\boldsymbol\epsilon-\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon,\ t\big)\big\rVert^2\Big]$$

Train one shared network on this single objective — pick a random clean clip, a random $t$, a random $\boldsymbol\epsilon$; corrupt; ask it to name the noise. At sampling time, start from $\mathbf{x}_T\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbf I)$ and step down using its predictions.

$$\mathbf{x}_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(\mathbf{x}_t-\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)\Big)+\sigma_t\,\mathbf{z},\qquad \mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbf I)$$

The score-matching view — the same thing from physics

Song & Ermon arrived at diffusion from a different door: score matching. The score of a distribution is the gradient of its log-density, $\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p(\mathbf x)$ — a vector field pointing "uphill" toward likely data. If you knew it, you could sample by following it (Langevin dynamics). The link to DDPM is exact: predicting the noise is estimating the score of the noise-perturbed data, up to a scale set by the noise level.

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0)=-\frac{\boldsymbol\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)\approx-\frac{\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}$$

Song's 2021 synthesis recast the whole thing as a stochastic differential equation: the forward process is an SDE that drifts the data toward noise, and generation solves the corresponding reverse-time SDE, whose drift is exactly the score. DDPM's discrete chain is one particular discretisation of that SDE. This is the unifying picture the rest of the field builds on.

derivation · from the variational bound to the simple loss

The true training target is the variational (ELBO) bound on $-\log p_\theta(\mathbf x_0)$, a sum of KL terms $D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(q(\mathbf x_{t-1}\mid\mathbf x_t,\mathbf x_0)\,\|\,p_\theta\big)$. Bayes' rule makes the forward posterior Gaussian with a closed-form mean

$$\tilde{\boldsymbol\mu}_t(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf x_0)=\frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\beta_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\mathbf x_0+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\mathbf x_t,\qquad \tilde\beta_t=\frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\beta_t.$$

Each KL between two Gaussians of equal covariance is just a scaled squared difference of means, $\propto\lVert\tilde{\boldsymbol\mu}_t-\boldsymbol\mu_\theta\rVert^2$. Substituting $\mathbf x_0=(\mathbf x_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ and matching terms shows the optimal $\boldsymbol\mu_\theta$ is the sampler formula above, and the residual is precisely $\lVert\boldsymbol\epsilon-\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta\rVert^2$ times a $t$-dependent weight. Ho et al.'s decisive empirical finding was that dropping that weight — training all timesteps equally — gives better samples. That is the "simple" loss: a maximum-likelihood objective wearing the clothes of a denoiser.

The oracle reverse trajectory in Demo 1 uses exactly $\tilde{\boldsymbol\mu}_t$ with the true $\mathbf x_0$ — a perfect $\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta$ — which is why it converges cleanly back to the tone. A trained network only approximates it.

3Noise schedules — the dial that controls destruction

The schedule $\{\beta_t\}$ is the one free design choice in the forward process, and it matters more than it looks. It decides how fast information is destroyed — and therefore how the model's limited capacity is spread across the noise levels it must learn. Get it wrong and you either waste steps on near-clean signals or annihilate the structure too early.

The original DDPM used a linear schedule, $\beta_t$ rising linearly from $10^{-4}$ to $0.02$. Nichol & Dhariwal showed it destroys information too quickly near the end: the last quarter of steps operate on what is already almost pure noise, doing little useful work. Their cosine schedule fixes this by defining $\bar\alpha_t$ directly so it decays gently and spends more steps in the perceptually critical middle range:

$$\bar\alpha_t=\frac{f(t)}{f(0)},\qquad f(t)=\cos^2\!\Big(\frac{t/T+s}{1+s}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\Big),\qquad s=0.008$$

Switch the schedule below and watch all three views update. The key diagnostic is the log-SNR, $\log\!\big(\bar\alpha_t/(1-\bar\alpha_t)\big)$: it measures how much signal survives relative to noise at each step. The cosine curve keeps log-SNR changing smoothly and lingers longer near the informative zero-crossing, whereas the linear schedule plunges through it.

Top: per-step variance β_t. Middle: cumulative signal retention ᾱ_t. Bottom: log-SNR — the schedule's true signature.

Read the middle panel. $\bar\alpha_t$ is the fraction of the clean signal's amplitude that survives to step $t$. Where the cosine curve stays high for longer, the model gets many training examples that are only mildly corrupted — the regime that fixes fine detail. The linear curve dumps that budget into near-pure-noise steps that teach the model almost nothing new.

4Diffusion for audio — DiffWave, WaveGrad and guidance

The 2020 image results landed on audio within months. Two vocoders arrived almost together — DiffWave and WaveGrad — both generating a raw waveform by denoising, both conditioned on a mel-spectrogram. They are diffusion's answer to the GAN vocoders of Part 3: given the mel that a TTS acoustic model produced, invent the matching audio.

ModelWhat it doesArchitectureNote
DiffWave Denoising diffusion on the raw waveform, conditioned on mel. Also works unconditionally and for class-conditional generation. Bidirectional dilated-conv stack (WaveNet-like), no autoregression — the whole clip is denoised in parallel per step. ~6 steps already usable; 50 steps near-transparent.
WaveGrad Estimates the data gradient (score) to refine noise into audio, conditioned on mel; conditions on a continuous noise level rather than a discrete index. UNet-style with FiLM conditioning on the mel and on $\sqrt{\bar\alpha}$. Trades steps for quality on a single trained model.

Conditioning — how the mel gets in

The mel-spectrogram enters the denoiser as a side input at every layer, typically upsampled to the audio rate and injected through FiLM / bias-scale modulation. Formally the model learns the conditional score $\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t,\mathbf c)$ with $\mathbf c$ the mel. Everything from Sections 1–2 carries over unchanged; the condition just rides along. This is why the same machinery serves TTS (mel from text), singing (Part 5's DiffSinger), and bandwidth extension.

Classifier-free guidance — trading diversity for fidelity

To make a conditional model follow its condition more tightly, classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans) trains one network to do both conditional and unconditional denoising (by randomly dropping $\mathbf c$), then at sampling time extrapolates away from the unconditional prediction:

$$\tilde{\boldsymbol\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t,\mathbf c)=\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t,\varnothing)+w\,\big(\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t,\mathbf c)-\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t,\varnothing)\big)$$

The guidance weight $w>1$ sharpens adherence to the prompt at some cost in diversity — the same knob that powers text-to-image and, in Parts 11–12, text-to-audio. It needs no separate classifier, which is why it became universal.

The cost that defines the rest of this part. Every DiffWave sample is many forward passes through a large network — DiffWave's parallelism across time is a huge win over WaveNet's per-sample autoregression, but it is still tens of network evaluations where a GAN vocoder needs one. Section 5 is about closing that gap.

5The step-count problem — and how to sample fast

Diffusion's Achilles heel is sampling speed. Quality is superb and mode coverage is excellent — it does not collapse the way a GAN can — but a naive DDPM sampler needs hundreds to a thousand sequential network evaluations. That is the same "sequential-slowness" theme that dogged the autoregressive models of Part 2, in a new costume. The last five years of diffusion research are largely one question: fewer steps, same quality.

Move the step count in the demo. With a good sampler, reconstruction error falls steeply for the first handful of steps and then flattens — a strong signal that most of the thousand-step budget is wasted. The wall-clock scales linearly with steps, so the engineering prize is obvious.

Left: reconstruction error vs steps (DDIM-style deterministic sampler). Right: the recovered signal at the chosen budget, over the clean target.

Four ways out

MethodIdeaTypical steps
DDIM A deterministic, non-Markovian sampler sharing the same trained network. Removes the injected noise $\sigma_t\mathbf z$, so you can skip timesteps and take a straight ODE path. 10–50
Progressive distillation Train a student to match two teacher steps in one, then halve again, repeatedly — each round doubles speed. 4–8
Consistency models Learn a network that maps any point on a noise trajectory directly to its origin, enabling one- or two-step generation. 1–2
Rectified flow / flow matching Train straight-line probability paths so an ODE solver needs very few steps; "reflow" straightens them further. 1–4

DDIM is the workhorse — same weights, a caption-worthy trick. The rest retrain or distil for extra speed. Consistency models and rectified flow are the current frontier, pushing audio diffusion toward the single-pass latency that GANs enjoyed all along, without the mode collapse.

derivation · why DDIM can skip steps

DDIM defines a family of non-Markovian forward processes that all share the same marginals $q(\mathbf x_t\mid\mathbf x_0)$ as DDPM — so a network trained with the simple loss is already a valid DDIM model, no retraining. Its update first forms the predicted clean signal (Tweedie's formula),

$$\hat{\mathbf x}_0=\frac{\mathbf x_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t)}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}},$$

then re-noises it to the next level on the subsequence with the model's own $\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta$ instead of fresh noise:

$$\mathbf x_{t-1}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\hat{\mathbf x}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t).$$

With the stochastic term set to zero this is a deterministic map — an Euler step of the probability-flow ODE. Because there is no re-injected randomness to integrate out, you can evaluate it on any decreasing subset of timesteps and still land near the data manifold; the discretisation error, not lost noise, is all that grows as you take fewer, larger steps. That is exactly the error curve the demo traces.

6Flows — exact likelihood and the road to flow matching

The "& Flow" in the title is not an afterthought — normalizing flows are diffusion's close cousin, and the two are converging. A flow is an invertible neural network $f$ that maps data to a simple base distribution (a Gaussian) and back. Because it is bijective with a tractable Jacobian, it gives something diffusion and GANs cannot: the exact log-likelihood, via the change-of-variables formula.

$$\log p(\mathbf x)=\log p_{\mathbf z}\big(f(\mathbf x)\big)+\log\Big|\det\frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf x}\Big|$$

WaveGlow (NVIDIA, 2019) is the canonical audio flow: a stack of invertible affine coupling layers, conditioned on the mel-spectrogram, that turns a Gaussian into a waveform in a single parallel forward pass — no autoregression, no iterative denoising. Trained purely by maximising the exact likelihood above, it sidesteps the adversarial instability of GAN vocoders and the many-step cost of diffusion. The price is architectural: invertibility with a cheap Jacobian is a real constraint, so flows tend to need many parameters for a given quality, and the affine-coupling trick limits how the network can mix its inputs.

Continuous flows meet diffusion

Push the number of coupling layers to a continuum and a flow becomes an ODE — a continuous normalizing flow — that transports the Gaussian to the data along a smooth trajectory. This is the bridge: diffusion's probability-flow ODE and a CNF are the same kind of object. Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) trains such an ODE directly by regressing its velocity field onto a chosen target path, and rectified flow chooses straight paths so the ODE integrates in a handful of steps:

$$\mathbf x_t=(1-t)\,\mathbf x_0+t\,\boldsymbol\epsilon,\qquad \mathcal L_\text{FM}=\mathbb E_{t,\mathbf x_0,\boldsymbol\epsilon}\big\lVert v_\theta(\mathbf x_t,t)-(\boldsymbol\epsilon-\mathbf x_0)\big\rVert^2$$

Notice the family resemblance to the diffusion loss of Section 2 — both regress a network onto a simple target under a linear interpolation of data and noise. Flow matching is increasingly the training objective of choice for new audio and music generators precisely because it inherits diffusion's stability and coverage while sampling in far fewer steps.

Diffusion vs flow, one line. Diffusion learns to reverse a stochastic corruption step by step; a flow learns a single deterministic invertible map. Flow matching unifies them: a deterministic ODE trained the diffusion way.
Where flows lost. Pure normalizing flows never matched diffusion's peak quality at equal size — the invertibility constraint is expensive. Their ideas survived not as standalone vocoders but folded into VITS (Part 6) and into the flow-matching objective now training the latest systems.

7Four years, static to state of the art

Diffusion went from an image-generation curiosity to the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity audio and music in about four years. Each line below is a step on the road from "beautiful but slow" to "beautiful and fast."

2020
DDPM (Ho et al.) makes denoising diffusion practical with the ε-prediction simple loss; DiffWave & WaveGrad bring it to audio as mel-conditioned vocoders. Part 8
2021
DDIM enables deterministic few-step sampling on trained models; Song et al.'s score SDE unifies diffusion and score matching; cosine schedules and classifier-free guidance sharpen quality. Part 8
2022
Progressive distillation compresses samplers to a handful of steps; latent diffusion moves the process into a VAE space, powering Riffusion and AudioLDM. Part 11
2023
